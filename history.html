THE STORY OF THE HIGHLANDS

A Heritage Carried Forward

Long before the Cordillera Administrative Region was established, Indigenous communities developed their own systems of agriculture, trade, customary governance, craftsmanship, and stewardship of ancestral lands.

Rice terraces, irrigation knowledge, weaving, woodcarving, oral histories, and community celebrations reflect the creativity and knowledge passed down through generations. These traditions differ among communities and continue to evolve in modern life.

On July 15, 1987, Executive Order No. 220 established the Cordillera Administrative Region. Today, CAR comprises Abra, Apayao, Benguet, Ifugao, Kalinga, and Mountain Province, with Baguio City as its regional center.

IMPORTANT MILESTONES

A Short Regional Timeline

Before colonization

Indigenous Highland Communities

Communities developed distinct agricultural systems, customary practices, arts, and relationships with their ancestral lands.

Colonial and modern eras

Change and Continuity

Political and economic changes affected highland communities, while local identities, knowledge, and traditions continued to develop.

July 15, 1987

Establishment of CAR

Executive Order No. 220 established the Cordillera Administrative Region as an administrative region of the Philippines.

Present day

A Living Heritage

Cordilleran communities continue to preserve cultural knowledge while participating in education, public life, agriculture, and tourism.

CELEBRATIONS AND TRADITIONS

Cultural Highlights

Festivals

  • Panagbenga: Baguio's annual flower festival.
  • Lang-ay Festival: A cultural celebration associated with Mountain Province.
  • Imbayah: A festival associated with Ifugao heritage.

Festival schedules and programs vary. Consult official local announcements before visiting.

Traditional Crafts and Food

  • Handwoven textiles created by local artisans.
  • Woodcarving and other traditional crafts.
  • Heirloom rice and highland vegetables.
  • Local coffee, strawberry products, and regional dishes.

Support local makers and respect the cultural meanings attached to community-specific designs.
